Local Landscaping Tips for Marlton Homeowners

A lot of Marlton's newer homes — particularly those built in the last 10 to 15 years — were sold with minimal topsoil over heavily compacted subsoil left from construction. If your lawn has never responded well to fertilizing or seeding, the soil is often the culprit. We recommend getting a basic soil test (available through Rutgers Cooperative Extension for Burlington County) before investing in a big lawn renovation. Knowing your soil's pH and nutrient profile lets us recommend exactly the right treatment and avoid spending money on products that won't help. In established Marlton neighborhoods, the main seasonal priority is usually leaf management in October and November — the heavy leaf drop from oaks and maples along Marlton's residential streets can smother a lawn in just a few weeks if left unattended.

Marlton's close proximity to the Pinelands means some properties — particularly those backing to wooded areas — deal with acidic, sandy soil rather than the typical South Jersey clay. Sandy soil drains fast and needs more frequent, lighter fertilizing. If your lawn looks thin and yellow despite regular care, a pH test could reveal the issue immediately.
